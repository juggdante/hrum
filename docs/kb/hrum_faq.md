# HRUM FAQ for HR leaders

This page explains how to read HRUM numbers. It describes this project's data and rules; it is not an
official City and County of San Francisco policy.

## Which data is real and which is synthetic?
Pay data is real: salaries, overtime, other salaries, retirement, health and dental, other benefits and total
compensation per pay record, from the public SF Employee Compensation dataset (fiscal years 2013 to 2026).
Department, organization group, job family, job title and employment type are also real.
Hire date, termination date, tenure, turnover, termination reason, level, performance rating, gender, age band
and work location are synthetic. They were generated by a program so the attrition use case can be demonstrated.
Any result that uses them shows what the platform can do; it is not a finding about San Francisco employees.

## What is a fiscal year?
The City's fiscal year runs from July 1 to June 30. FY2026 means July 2025 to June 2026. The source also has a
calendar-year view of the same pay; HRUM uses fiscal years only, so totals are never double counted.

## Why does HRUM count "pay records" instead of people?
The source has one row per employee per job per year and no stable employee id. A person who held two jobs in a
year appears twice. The headcount metric therefore counts paid pay records (total salary above zero). It is a
close proxy for headcount, not an exact count of people.

## Why are some years missing hours or employment type?
The source did not publish hours worked or employment type before FY2017. Full-time status and any per-hour
figure are only available from FY2017 onwards.

## Why are some groups missing from an answer?
To protect privacy, any group with fewer than 5 records is suppressed. For example, a job title held by three
people will not appear in a breakdown by job. HRUM also never answers questions about a named individual,
even though the public source contains names.

## What does compa-ratio mean here?
Compa-ratio compares an employee's salary with the median salary of everyone in the same job code. A value of
1.00 means paid at the internal median, 0.90 means 10% below it. It is an internal benchmark only; HRUM has no
external market salary survey yet.

## How is cost of turnover estimated?
Cost of turnover is an assumption, not a measured cost: 33% of the annual salary of each employee who left.
It is calculated on synthetic turnover data.

## How does the attrition risk model work?
An XGBoost model is trained on the synthetic FY2026 snapshot to predict whether an employee left during the year.
Inputs are tenure at the start of the year, compa-ratio, overtime ratio, performance rating, level, employment
type, age band and organization group. Gender is deliberately not used as a model input. Explanations use SHAP
values, which show how much each input pushed the predicted risk up or down. Because the training labels are
synthetic, the model demonstrates the method; its drivers mirror the rules used to generate the data.

## How can I trust a number in an answer?
Every number in an HRUM answer must come from a tool result that is shown with the answer: the metric
definition, the SQL that ran and its rows. An automatic check compares each number in the answer with the cited
evidence. If a number cannot be matched, the claim is removed, and if nothing verifiable remains the assistant
says it cannot answer.

## Who can see what?
The CHRO role can use governed metrics and model insights only. The analyst role can additionally run read-only
aggregate SQL on the mart tables. No role can see person-level rows or names.
